Crisis plan

You wrote most of this when you were steadier than you are now. That version of you gets a vote.

Step 1

Stop where you are

Feet still. Hands empty. Don't act on it for the length of this page — that's the only commitment right now.

  • S — Stop. Freeze the moment.
  • T — Take a step back, physically if you can.
  • O — Observe. What's happening in the room, in your body, in the thought.
  • P — Proceed mindfully. Pick the next step from this page, not from the urge.
Step 2

Put distance between you and harm

Move away from anything you could use to hurt yourself. Hand it to someone, put it somewhere that takes real effort to reach, or leave the room and don't go back alone. Distance buys time, and urges lose height with time.

If you're alone, go where other people are — a different room, a stairwell, outside the door, a shop that's open.

Step 3

Bring the body down first

The thinking won't come back until the physiology does. Pick one and do it for a few minutes:

  • Move hard. Fast walk, stairs, push-ups, dancing to something loud — enough to breathe heavily. Short and intense.
  • Paced breathing. Breathe out longer than you breathe in. Roughly in for 4, out for 6 to 8. Keep going past the point where it feels pointless.
  • Paired muscle relaxation. Tense a muscle group hard on the in-breath, release it completely on the long out-breath. Work up the body.
Step 4

Get through the next hour — ACCEPTS

  • Activities — something with your hands that needs a little attention.
  • Contributing — do one small thing for someone else.
  • Comparisons — a time you got through something like this.
  • Emotions — put on something that makes you feel a different thing on purpose.
  • Pushing away — box it, shelve it, set a time to come back to it.
  • Thoughts — count, name things, follow a plot.
  • Sensations — sound, texture, smell, taste. Nothing that hurts.

Then self-soothe through the five senses, gently: something warm to drink, a good smell, a familiar album, soft fabric, a view out a window.

Step 5

Pros and cons, honestly

Not the sanitised version. What acting on the urge would give you in the next ten minutes, what it would cost you tomorrow, next month, and with the people in step 6. Write it in a mood entry if it helps to see it.

Step 6

Tell someone it's bad right now

You don't have to explain it well. "It's bad tonight, can you stay on the phone" is a complete sentence.
